## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial user groups that will guide Kairos
product research.

These groups are not intended to represent every person who may
benefit from Kairos.

They provide an initial framework for identifying differences in:

- Computer-use context
- Responsibilities
- Independence
- Attention-management needs
- Time-management needs
- Support preferences
- Privacy expectations
- Guardian involvement
- Accessibility needs

The groups defined here should be treated as research hypotheses.

User research may reveal that different segmentation is more useful.

---

## 2. Research Philosophy

Kairos is designed with neurodivergent experiences in mind.

However, research participation should not require a diagnosis of
ADHD, autism, or another condition.

Research should focus primarily on the user's experiences and needs.

A participant may identify as:

- Neurodivergent
- Neurotypical
- Diagnosed with a specific condition
- Self-identified as neurodivergent
- Undiagnosed
- Unsure
- Someone who does not identify as neurodivergent

The relevant research question is not simply:

> Does this person have ADHD?

More useful questions include:

> Does this person struggle to determine what to do first?

> Do they lose track of their original intention after becoming
> distracted?

> Do they lose awareness of time while deeply focused?

> Do they struggle to transition between activities?

> What type of support helps them?

Kairos should be designed around validated needs rather than
diagnostic assumptions.

---

## 3. Initial Research Cohorts

The initial research strategy will focus on three broad cohorts.

These cohorts are primarily separated by life context and degree of
independence rather than diagnosis.

The initial cohorts are:

1. Student users, approximately ages 10 to 17
2. College and early-career users, approximately ages 18 to 24
3. Working adults, approximately ages 25 and older

These age ranges are research boundaries rather than strict product
boundaries.

---

## 4. Cohort A: Student Users

### Approximate Age

10 to 17

### Primary Context

Student users may use the same computer for:

- School assignments
- Research
- Online classes
- Communication
- Games
- Videos
- Social interaction
- Creative activities
- Personal interests

This creates an environment where required activities and highly
engaging alternatives may exist immediately beside one another.

### Potential Responsibilities

Responsibilities may include:

- Homework
- Studying
- School projects
- Online assignments
- Household responsibilities
- Extracurricular activities
- Personal routines

Some responsibilities may be externally structured by:

- Parents or guardians
- Teachers
- Schools
- Coaches
- Other adults

### Initial Research Interests

Research with this cohort should explore:

- How students decide what schoolwork to complete first
- How students remember unfinished assignments
- How distractions affect homework completion
- How students experience time while gaming or watching videos
- How students respond to reminders
- How students transition away from enjoyable activities
- How much structure students want from Kairos
- How students want Kairos to communicate with them
- How visual customization affects willingness to use the product
- How guardian involvement affects trust and autonomy

### Guardian Considerations

This cohort introduces additional questions involving:

- Consent
- Privacy
- Account ownership
- Guardian permissions
- Monitoring
- Restrictions
- Data collection
- Data retention
- Safety

Guardian functionality should not automatically imply surveillance.

Research should explore how Kairos can support younger users while
preserving appropriate autonomy and trust.

### Assumptions Requiring Validation

We currently hypothesize that:

- Students may benefit from stronger transition assistance when
  switching between recreation and responsibilities.
- Customization may increase willingness to keep Kairos active.
- Students may want different communication styles depending on
  activity.
- Guardian-controlled restrictions may create trust concerns if they
  are not transparent.
- Schoolwork may require applications or websites that could
  otherwise appear distracting.

These are hypotheses, not established findings.

## 5. Cohort B: College and Early-Career Users

### Approximate Age

18 to 24

### Primary Context

Users in this cohort may experience significantly more independence
than they had during earlier education.

They may be responsible for independently managing:

- Classes
- Assignments
- Work
- Appointments
- Household responsibilities
- Social activities
- Personal projects
- Sleep
- Recreation

External structure may be reduced while the number of competing
responsibilities increases.

### Potential Responsibilities

Responsibilities may include:

- Coursework
- Studying
- Long-term assignments
- Employment
- Job searching
- Professional development
- Household tasks
- Appointments
- Financial responsibilities
- Personal projects

### Initial Research Interests

Research with this cohort should explore:

- How users prioritize competing responsibilities
- How users manage long-term assignments
- How vague tasks become actionable
- How users estimate task duration
- How distractions affect planned work
- How users recover after losing focus
- How hyperfocus affects schedules
- How users respond when plans fall behind
- How much intervention users want
- Whether users want Kairos to learn from previous behavior

### Independence Considerations

Users in this cohort may have greater responsibility for creating
their own structure.

Research should explore whether Kairos is most useful as:

- A companion
- An assistant
- A coach
- A configurable combination

The product should not assume that increased independence means the
user requires less support.

### Assumptions Requiring Validation

We currently hypothesize that:

- Reduced external structure may increase the value of planning
  assistance.
- Users may struggle to estimate how long large assignments require.
- Schedule recovery may be particularly valuable when one activity
  takes longer than expected.
- Users may want different focus strengths for different activities.
- Personalized time estimates may become more useful than generic
  estimates.

These are hypotheses, not established findings.

---

## 6. Cohort C: Working Adults

### Approximate Age

25 and older

### Primary Context

Working adults may need to balance multiple areas of responsibility
within the same day.

These may include:

- Employment
- Meetings
- Household responsibilities
- Parenting or caregiving
- Appointments
- Personal projects
- Education
- Recreation
- Rest

Remote and hybrid work may further combine professional and personal
activities within the same physical and digital environment.

### Potential Responsibilities

Responsibilities may include:

- Focused work
- Meetings
- Administrative tasks
- Communication
- Deadlines
- Household tasks
- Family responsibilities
- Appointments
- Personal projects
- Continuing education

### Initial Research Interests

Research with this cohort should explore:

- How users determine what deserves attention first
- How meetings interrupt focused work
- How users return to work after interruptions
- How deep focus affects upcoming commitments
- How users balance work and personal responsibilities
- How fatigue affects planning
- How users respond when schedules become unrealistic
- Whether users want work and personal profiles
- How much activity awareness users consider acceptable
- How privacy expectations affect adoption

### Work Context Considerations

Research should distinguish between:

- Office-based work
- Remote work
- Hybrid work
- Freelance work
- Self-employment
- Shift-based work

Different work environments may create substantially different
requirements.

Kairos should not assume that all working adults follow a traditional
weekday office schedule.

### Assumptions Requiring Validation

We currently hypothesize that:

- Transition support may be valuable before meetings and
  appointments.
- Remote workers may experience greater overlap between productive
  and distracting computer activities.
- Users may want stronger separation between work and personal
  contexts.
- Privacy expectations may increase as activity-awareness
  capabilities become more advanced.
- Hyperfocus may create problems even when the activity itself is
  productive.

These are hypotheses, not established findings.

## 7. Cross-Cohort Support Needs

Age and life context alone are unlikely to explain what type of Kairos
experience a user needs.

Research should therefore examine support needs across all cohorts.

A user may experience one or several of the following.

---

### Prioritization Support

The user knows several things need to be completed but struggles to
determine what deserves attention first.

Example:

> I have five things to do and don't know where to start.

---

### Attention Recovery Support

The user becomes distracted and loses awareness of their original
intention.

Example:

> I opened this website for one thing and somehow I've been here for
> 45 minutes.

---

### Transition Support

The user knows another activity requires attention but has difficulty
disengaging from the current activity.

Example:

> I know I need to stop, but I can't pull myself away from what I'm
> doing.

---

### Time Awareness Support

The user has difficulty recognizing how much time has passed or how
much remains.

Example:

> I didn't realize two hours had passed.

---

### Task Initiation Support

The user understands what needs to be completed but struggles to
begin.

Example:

> I know exactly what I need to do. I just can't get started.

---

### Planning Support

The user understands the desired outcome but struggles to identify the
steps required to reach it.

Example:

> I know what I need to finish, but I don't know how to break it down.

---

### Schedule Recovery Support

The user's original plan becomes unrealistic and they struggle to
determine how to reorganize the remaining time.

Example:

> One thing took longer than expected and now I don't know what to do
> with the rest of my day.

---

## 8. Support Intensity

Research should also examine how much assistance users want from
Kairos.

Initial conceptual levels include:

### Low Support

The user primarily wants ambient awareness and lightweight reminders.

### Moderate Support

The user wants reminders and progressive intervention when attention
or timing begins to drift.

### High Support

The user wants Kairos to participate more actively in maintaining
structure and supporting transitions.

### Strict Support

The user intentionally delegates stronger intervention capabilities
to Kairos, potentially including restrictions during selected focus
sessions.

These labels are research terminology.

They should not be assumed to represent final user-facing language.

---

## 9. Support Need Is Not Ability

The amount of support a user wants should not be interpreted as a
measure of intelligence, maturity, capability, or independence.

A highly capable professional may want Strict Focus.

A younger student may prefer Low Support.

A user may also require different support levels depending on the
activity.

For example:

```text
Writing Report
Support: High

Team Meeting
Support: Low

Personal Coding Project
Support: Moderate

Deadline Work
Support: Strict
```

Kairos should eventually support situational differences rather than
assigning a permanent support level to the user.

---

## 10. Age and Experience

Age remains relevant to Kairos, but it should not automatically
determine the user experience.

Age may influence requirements involving:

- Consent
- Guardian involvement
- Privacy
- Safety
- Account management
- Language complexity
- Legal requirements

Age should not automatically determine:

- Theme
- Communication style
- Gamification
- Animation
- Focus strength
- Support intensity

Research should validate how these factors interact rather than
assuming preferences based on age.

---

## 11. Overlapping User Contexts

The research cohorts should not be treated as mutually exclusive
personas.

A single person may simultaneously be:

- A working adult
- A college student
- A parent
- A caregiver
- A hobbyist
- A remote worker

Kairos should eventually respond to context rather than forcing the
user into one permanent category.

## 13. Initial Research Priorities

Early research should attempt to answer:

1. Which attention-management problems occur most frequently?
2. Which problems create the greatest negative consequences?
3. What strategies do users currently use?
4. Where do existing strategies fail?
5. Which interventions feel helpful?
6. Which interventions feel annoying or controlling?
7. How do support needs change by activity?
8. How much computer activity awareness feels acceptable?
9. How important is visual and behavioral customization?
10. How should Kairos communicate when attention drifts?
11. How should Kairos handle hyperfocus?
12. How should guardian involvement work for younger users?
13. What would cause users to stop using Kairos?
14. What would make users trust Kairos?

These questions will be expanded in:

`RESEARCH_QUESTIONS.md`

## 14. Recruitment Considerations

Future research recruitment should attempt to include variation in:

- Age
- Education
- Employment
- Computer experience
- Neurodivergent identity
- Diagnosis status
- Support needs
- Work environment
- Accessibility needs
- Preferred productivity strategies

Research should avoid recruiting only highly technical users.

Kairos is intended to support people with varying levels of computer
experience.

---

## 15. Research Ethics

Research involving younger users requires additional care.

Before conducting research with minors, the project should establish
appropriate procedures involving:

- Guardian consent
- Participant assent
- Data minimization
- Privacy
- Recording
- Data storage
- Participant withdrawal
- Compensation
- Research questions appropriate to the participant's age

Kairos should not collect sensitive diagnostic or medical information
unless that information is genuinely necessary for a defined research
question.

---

## 16. Open Questions

The following questions remain unresolved:

- Are the initial age cohorts the most useful segmentation?
- Should college students and early-career workers remain one cohort?
- How should caregiver responsibilities affect segmentation?
- Should remote workers become a dedicated research group?
- How should users with significant accessibility needs be
  represented?
- How should guardian-supported accounts operate?
- How much support should Kairos provide outside the computer?
- How frequently do support needs change by activity?
- Which support needs should determine onboarding?

These questions should remain open until research provides sufficient
evidence to make stronger decisions.

---

## 17. Current Research Position

The three initial research cohorts are:

1. Student users, approximately ages 10 to 17
2. College and early-career users, approximately ages 18 to 24
3. Working adults, approximately ages 25 and older

These groups provide an initial recruitment and analysis framework.

They should not be treated as final personas or permanent product
segments.

The next research step is to define the specific user needs,
assumptions, and research questions that must be validated across
these groups.
