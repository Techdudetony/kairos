## 1. Product Overview

Kairos is an adaptive desktop companion designed to help users improve time management, maintain focus, organize activities, manage transitions, and make more intentional use of their time on a computer.

Kairos is designed with neurodivergent users in mind, beginning with users approximately age 10 and older. However, Kairos does not require a diagnosis and should remain useful to anyone who benefits from additional support with executive functioning, focus, organization, routines, or time awareness.

Rather than functioning solely as a traditional planner, timer, calendar, or task manager, Kairos is intended to become an optional support layer within the user's desktop environment.

Through configurable widgets, reminders, focus sessions, routines, overlays, interventions, and future adaptive planning capabilities, Kairos works alongside the user while they use their computer.

The user remains in control of how visible, active, strict, and aware Kairos is allowed to become.

---

## 2. Vision Statement

> **Kairos aims to create a computer experience that adapts to how an individual manages attention, time, and responsibilities, helping them focus on what matters at the right moment without making them feel controlled by their technology.**

Kairos should help users spend less energy remembering what they intended to do, determining what comes next, recovering from distractions, and managing transitions between activities.

The long-term vision is for Kairos to become a personalized companion that understands how the individual user works and provides the appropriate amount of assistance at the appropriate time.

---

## 3. Why "Kairos"?

Kairos is inspired by the ancient Greek concept of **kairos**, commonly associated with the appropriate, opportune, or right moment.

This meaning reflects the central purpose of the product.

Kairos is not simply concerned with how much time has passed.

It is concerned with helping the user understand:

- What matters right now?
- What is coming next?
- When should I begin?
- When should I stop?
- When should I transition?
- When should I take a break?
- What should I do when my original plan no longer works?

The name represents a shift from simply tracking time toward helping users make meaningful decisions about how they use it.

---

## 4. The Problem

Computers provide access to work, education, communication, entertainment, creativity, and information within the same environment.

This flexibility can also create significant challenges for users who experience difficulty with:

- Time awareness
- Task initiation
- Sustained attention
- Distraction
- Working memory
- Prioritization
- Task switching
- Planning
- Estimating task duration
- Maintaining routines
- Recovering when plans change

Many existing productivity applications require the user to remember to open the productivity application, review their responsibilities, determine what to do, begin the activity, monitor their own progress, and respond appropriately when distracted.

For users who already experience difficulty with executive functioning, the tools intended to help organize their responsibilities may themselves require significant executive functioning to use effectively.

Kairos seeks to reduce this friction.

---

## 5. Product Promise

Kairos should help users answer three questions throughout their computer experience:

> **What am I doing now?**

> **What should I be aware of?**

> **What comes next?**

When those answers become unclear, Kairos should help the user recover rather than punish them for becoming distracted, falling behind, or changing their plans.

---

## 6. Target Audience

### 6.1 Primary Audience

Kairos is initially intended for users approximately age 10 and older who benefit from additional support with:

- Focus
- Time management
- Organization
- Task initiation
- Task completion
- Transitions
- Routines
- Planning
- Working memory
- Distraction management

The product should be designed particularly with neurodivergent experiences in mind.

Potential users may include people with ADHD, autism, executive-function challenges, learning differences, or other cognitive differences.

A diagnosis should never be required to use or benefit from Kairos.

### 6.2 Age-Inclusive Experience

Kairos should not assume that age alone determines how an interface should look or behave.

A 12-year-old may prefer a minimal interface.

A 25-year-old may prefer a playful and highly gamified interface.

A professional may prefer a calming nature-inspired environment.

A student may prefer a highly structured and direct experience.

Kairos should therefore personalize the experience primarily around individual preferences and support needs rather than simply assigning users a "child" or "adult" interface.

Age may still affect features where privacy, guardian involvement, consent, safety, or legal requirements make age relevant.

---

## 7. Desired User Outcomes

Kairos should help users:

- Improve awareness of how they use their time.
- Better manage scheduled activities and responsibilities.
- Remain focused on intended activities.
- Reduce the impact of unwanted distractions.
- Begin tasks with less friction.
- Transition between activities more effectively.
- Break overwhelming goals into manageable actions.
- Make more intentional use of available time.
- Complete more of the activities that matter to them.
- Recover when schedules or plans no longer work.
- Balance focused activity with appropriate breaks and recovery.

Success should not be defined by maximizing the amount of time a user spends working.

Kairos should instead help users use their available time intentionally and effectively.

### Desired User Sentiment

A successful Kairos user should eventually be able to say:

> "Kairos helps me manage my time, stay focused, and accomplish what I want to do without making me feel controlled by my computer."

---

## 8. Core Experience

Kairos should operate as a configurable desktop companion rather than an application that must constantly remain open in the foreground.

Depending on user preferences and permissions, Kairos may interact through:

- Desktop widgets
- System notifications
- Focus timers
- Visual time indicators
- Scheduled reminders
- Transition warnings
- Routines
- Screen overlays
- Full-screen interventions
- Application or website restrictions
- Quick-capture tools
- Contextual assistance

The amount of intervention should depend on the user's selected preferences and the context of the activity.

---

## 9. Progressive Intervention

Kairos should use **progressive intervention** as the default approach when helping users recover from distraction or prepare for important activities.

Rather than immediately interrupting or restricting the user, Kairos may progressively increase the visibility and urgency of assistance.

A potential progression may include:

1. Ambient awareness
2. Gentle reminder
3. Active reminder
4. Screen overlay
5. Strong intervention
6. User-authorized restrictions

The exact progression will be defined during UX and technical design.

### User Authority

The user ultimately controls how much authority Kairos receives.

Users should eventually be able to configure focus experiences such as:

- Standard Focus
- Enhanced Focus
- Strict Focus
- Custom Focus

Strict modes may permit Kairos to temporarily restrict user-selected applications or websites.

Kairos should not assume this authority without explicit permission.

> **Kairos may become increasingly difficult to ignore, but the amount of control it receives must be intentionally delegated by the user.**

---

## 10. Adaptive Time Management

Kairos should support two forms of time management.

### 10.1 Scheduled Time Management

Kairos should help users follow activities they have already planned.

Examples include:

- Classes
- Homework
- Work sessions
- Meetings
- Appointments
- Household responsibilities
- Personal projects
- Recreation
- Breaks
- Morning routines
- Evening routines

Kairos should help users prepare for transitions before activities begin rather than relying exclusively on reminders at the scheduled start time.

### 10.2 Assisted Planning

Future versions of Kairos should help users transform vague intentions into actionable plans.

For example:

> "I need to clean my room."

may become:

- Pick up trash
- Collect dirty clothes
- Put away clean clothes
- Organize the desk
- Put misplaced items away
- Make the bed
- Vacuum the floor

Kairos may then help estimate the time required and organize those actions around the user's available schedule.

---

## 11. Personalized Time Understanding

A long-term goal for Kairos is to learn how the individual user experiences time.

Generic estimates should provide a starting point rather than becoming permanent assumptions.

Kairos may eventually learn from:

- Initial estimates
- User-adjusted estimates
- Planned duration
- Actual active time
- Total elapsed time
- Break duration
- Interruptions
- Task complexity
- Environmental or task context
- User feedback
- Previous completion history

For example, "clean my room" may require significantly different amounts of time depending on whether the room requires a light cleanup or a major cleaning session.

Future versions of Kairos may learn these relationships and ask relevant contextual questions before recommending time allocations.

This capability may eventually form a **Personal Time Model** for each user.

> **Kairos should learn how the individual user experiences time rather than expecting the user to conform to generic estimates of how long activities should take.**

---

## 12. Activity Awareness

Kairos should use a graduated activity-awareness model.

Users should determine how much information Kairos is permitted to observe about their computer activity.

Potential levels include:

### Level 1: Session Awareness

Kairos knows that a focus session is active but does not monitor applications or websites.

### Level 2: Application Awareness

Kairos may identify the currently active application.

### Level 3: Application and Domain Awareness

Kairos may identify active applications and website domains without necessarily inspecting page contents.

### Level 4: Context Awareness

When explicitly enabled, Kairos may use additional context to determine whether current computer activity appears relevant to the user's intended task.

Increasing awareness may enable more intelligent assistance, but advanced monitoring should never be required for basic Kairos functionality.

> **Kairos should only know what it needs to know to provide the level of assistance the user has requested.**

---

## 13. Privacy Philosophy

Kairos should follow a privacy-first approach.

Users should understand:

- What Kairos can observe
- Why Kairos needs that information
- How the information is used
- Where the information is processed
- Where the information is stored
- How long information is retained
- How permissions can be revoked

Where technically feasible, activity analysis and behavioral processing should occur locally on the user's device.

Information should not be transmitted to external services merely because Kairos is capable of collecting it.

Detailed privacy, security, youth-safety, and data-retention requirements will be established separately.

---

## 14. Companion Experience

Kairos should not enforce a single personality.

Instead, users should be able to configure how the companion communicates with them.

Kairos may function as a combination of:

### Companion

Works alongside the user and helps maintain awareness of intentions and responsibilities.

### Assistant

Quietly organizes information, schedules, tasks, routines, reminders, and transitions.

### Coach

Provides greater accountability, encouragement, progress feedback, and active intervention when requested.

The user should determine the preferred balance between these roles.

---

## 15. Communication Styles

Potential communication styles may include:

- Calm
- Direct
- Encouraging
- Playful
- Minimal

Communication style should remain independent from intervention strength.

For example, a user may prefer:

- Calm communication with Strict Focus
- Direct communication with Standard Focus
- Playful communication with Enhanced Focus
- Minimal communication with Strict Focus

A supportive tone should not automatically mean weaker intervention.

Likewise, stronger intervention should not require harsh or punitive communication.

---

## 16. Recovery-Oriented Design

Kairos should assume that plans sometimes fail.

Users may:

- Become distracted
- Underestimate task duration
- Miss scheduled activities
- Lose motivation
- Experience interruptions
- Become overwhelmed
- Need additional breaks
- Change priorities
- Abandon an original plan

These situations should be treated as opportunities to assist the user rather than failures that deserve punishment.

Kairos should avoid unnecessary:

- Guilt
- Shame
- Failure messaging
- Punitive streak systems
- Red warning overload
- Negative reinforcement

Instead, Kairos should help answer:

> What should we do now?

Future features such as **Rescue Mode** may help users reorganize the remainder of their schedule when the original plan is no longer realistic.

## 17. Customization Philosophy

Customization in Kairos should extend beyond visual themes.

Users should eventually be able to personalize:

### Appearance

- Themes
- Colors
- Typography
- Widget styles
- Transparency
- Animation
- Layout
- Visual density

### Communication

- Communication style
- Message length
- Encouragement level
- Notification language

### Focus

- Default focus strength
- Intervention progression
- Restricted applications
- Restricted websites
- Break behavior

### Awareness

- Activity-awareness level
- Sensitive applications
- Sensitive websites
- Monitoring exclusions

### Sensory Experience

- Sounds
- Animation intensity
- Motion reduction
- Visual effects
- Notification intensity

Themes may include experiences such as a cherry-blossom-inspired **Sakura** theme alongside professional, minimal, nature, dark, playful, and other visual experiences.

## 18. Product Principles

Detailed product principles will be maintained in [PRODUCT_PRINCIPLES.md](PRODUCT_PRINCIPLES.md).

The initial vision establishes several foundational principles:

1. **Support, do not punish.**
2. **The user remains in control.**
3. **Progressive intervention should be the default.**
4. **Greater capability requires greater permission.**
5. **Need and preference should shape the experience more than age.**
6. **Privacy should increase alongside system awareness.**
7. **Kairos should adapt to the user rather than expecting the user to adapt to Kairos.**
8. **Recovery is part of productivity.**
9. **Breaks and transitions are legitimate parts of effective time management.**
10. **AI should assist the experience rather than become the purpose of the product.**

---

## 19. Long-Term Vision

Kairos should evolve from a customizable desktop focus and organization companion into an adaptive system capable of understanding how an individual user plans, focuses, transitions, and experiences time.

Over time, Kairos may become capable of:

- Recognizing personal time-management patterns
- Improving task-duration estimates
- Suggesting realistic schedules
- Breaking vague goals into actionable steps
- Identifying patterns associated with distraction
- Preparing users for upcoming transitions
- Adjusting recommendations based on context
- Helping recover from disrupted schedules
- Adapting communication to different situations
- Providing meaningful insights without encouraging unhealthy productivity

These capabilities should develop gradually and should remain transparent and controllable by the user.

---

## 20. Vision Boundary

Kairos is intended to support organization, focus, time management, and executive-function-related needs.

Kairos is **not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or determine the presence of ADHD, autism, or any other medical, developmental, psychological, or neurological condition.**

Users should not need a diagnosis to use Kairos.

The product should focus on the user's expressed needs rather than attempting to determine why those needs exist.

---

## 21. Definition of Product Success

Kairos will be successful when users feel that their computer has become easier to manage rather than another source of cognitive overhead.

Success means users can more consistently:

- Understand what they intended to do
- Begin what matters
- Maintain focus when desired
- Recognize when their attention has shifted
- Understand how much time remains
- Prepare for upcoming transitions
- Recover when plans change
- Make realistic decisions about their available time
- Complete meaningful activities
- Stop and rest when appropriate

Ultimately, Kairos should help users feel more capable of directing their time and attention while preserving their autonomy.

## 22. Current Product Stage

Kairos is currently in **Product Discovery and Planning**.

No technology stack or implementation architecture should be considered final at this stage.

Current priorities are:

1. Establish the product vision.
2. Define product principles.
3. Define the problem space.
4. Establish goals and non-goals.
5. Research target users and their needs.
6. Establish accessibility, privacy, and youth-safety requirements.
7. Define product requirements.
8. Design the core user experience.
9. Evaluate technical approaches.
10. Define the MVP.
11. Convert approved requirements into an actionable development backlog.

Implementation decisions should follow product and user requirements rather than determine them.
